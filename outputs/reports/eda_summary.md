# EDA Summary: Customer Revenue Intelligence

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** This summary is generated by `src/eda.py` from `data/processed/customer_sales_cleaned.csv`, the Step 3 output. Every number is computed; none is typed by hand. Re-running the script reproduces this file.

## 1. EDA Objective
The aim is to understand how revenue and profit are generated before any modelling:
- revenue trends over time;
- which products, categories, regions and customers matter most;
- how discounts relate to profitability;
- which questions the RFM, segmentation, CLV, prediction and forecasting steps should answer.

The analysis is exploratory and descriptive. Associations are reported as associations, not as causes.

**Measurement rules**
- Revenue, cost, profit and units count **Delivered order lines only**.
- An order is a distinct `Order_ID`. This was verified: 13,251 Order_IDs equal 13,251 distinct customer-date pairs, and no Order_ID has more than one customer, date or payment method.
- Order KPIs count only orders with at least one Delivered line.
- Customer age is summarised per customer.

## 4. Major Revenue Insights
- **Revenue is growing, but growth is slowing.** 2023 ₹1.31 Cr -> 2024 ₹1.98 Cr (+51.4%) -> 2025 ₹2.72 Cr (+37.7%).  
  *Implication:* Forecasting should model trend plus seasonality; the slowdown is worth diagnosing (fewer new customers or lower frequency?)
- **9.4% of booked sales never became revenue.** Lost to cancellations and returns: ₹62.55 L; COD cancel rate 6.2% vs UPI 3.5%.  
  *Implication:* Nudging COD users to prepaid methods could reduce leakage

## 5. Customer Insights
- **Revenue is concentrated in a minority of customers.** Top 20% -> 64.3%, top 10% -> 45.5%, top 1% -> 9.0%.  
  *Implication:* Losing a few top customers would hurt disproportionately; they are the priority for retention (RFM/CLV in Steps 5-7)
- **Frequent buyers drive revenue.** Customers with 11+ orders: 16.2% of buyers, 52.1% of revenue; customer revenue vs order count Spearman rho = 0.842.  
  *Implication:* Growing purchase frequency is a stronger lever than acquiring one-off buyers
- **A large share of customers has gone quiet.** 688 of 1984 buyers (34.7%) have not bought in over 180 days.  
  *Implication:* Reactivation candidates to size in RFM (Step 5); descriptive only here
- **Age is linked to spend; gender and region are not.** Median customer revenue: 35-49 ₹22,012 vs 18-24 ₹9,023 (Kruskal-Wallis p<0.001); gender p=0.81, region p=0.42.  
  *Implication:* Age band is a candidate feature for prediction; gender/region-based targeting has little support

## 6. Product Insights
- **Electronics dominates revenue but not profit.** Electronics = 50.1% of revenue, 23.3% of profit, margin 10.9%.  
  *Implication:* Revenue KPIs alone overstate Electronics' contribution; judge categories on profit
- **Fashion is the profit engine, but has the highest return rate.** Fashion = 20.1% of revenue, 36.8% of profit (margin 42.8%); return rate 10.9% vs 4.1% max elsewhere.  
  *Implication:* Reducing Fashion returns (sizing guides, fit tools) could protect the highest-margin revenue

## 9. Discount Insights
- **Deeper discounts go with lower margins in every category, while quantity barely changes.** Within-category Spearman rho(discount, margin): -0.89 to -0.37; rho(discount, quantity) = 0.06.  
  *Implication:* Discounts appear to cost margin without clearly lifting volume; test targeted rather than blanket discounts (association, not causation)
- **The overall discount-margin table is misleading because of category mix.** Margin by bucket (all categories): 0% 29.3%, 5-10% 19.4%, 15-20% 21.6%, 25-30% 21.4%, 35-40% 20.4%; the 5-10% bucket is heavy in low-margin Electronics.  
  *Implication:* Always compare discount effects within a category (Simpson's paradox)

## 10. Time-Series Insights
- **October is the peak month every year; Oct-Dec are always above average.** October revenue: 2023 ₹18.48 L, 2024 ₹27.95 L, 2025 ₹30.98 L; Q4 share of year: 2023 37.1%, 2024 35.2%, 2025 29.7%.  
  *Implication:* Plan inventory, staffing and marketing budget around the festive quarter
- **The festive peak is the least profitable month.** October margin 15.3%-16.9% vs overall 23.3%.  
  *Implication:* Festive growth is being bought with discounts; review the depth of festive discounts on thin-margin items
- **Weekends sell more.** Revenue per calendar day: Saturday 1.16x and Monday 0.89x the average day.  
  *Implication:* Schedule campaigns and flash sales for Friday-Sunday

## 11. Important Relationships (correlation ≠ causation)
Spearman correlations with |rho| ≥ 0.4, computed on Delivered lines:
- Sales vs Cost: Spearman rho = 0.98 (strong positive)
- Unit_Price vs Cost: Spearman rho = 0.91 (strong positive)
- Unit_Price vs Sales: Spearman rho = 0.89 (strong positive)
- Sales vs Profit: Spearman rho = 0.78 (strong positive)
- Cost vs Profit: Spearman rho = 0.68 (moderate positive)
- Unit_Price vs Profit: Spearman rho = 0.62 (moderate positive)

Most of these relationships are true by construction. Sales, Cost and Unit_Price move together because Sales = Qty × Price × (1 − Discount). They are not evidence that one variable drives another. Customer age shows only negligible correlation with any line-level amount. At customer level, however, age band is significantly associated with total revenue. Customers aged 35-49 place a median of 6 orders, against 4 for 18-24. Their median AOV is ₹3,535, against ₹2,686. The gap therefore comes mainly from purchase frequency, not from the value of individual lines.

## 13. Potential Business Problems
1. **Margin dilution from revenue mix.** Electronics accounts for 50.1% of revenue at a 10.9% margin. Some high-selling SKUs (the TV and the premium laptop) earn close to zero or negative profit.
2. **Discount-driven festive growth.** The October peak comes with the lowest monthly margins.
3. **Customer concentration and lapsing.** Revenue depends on a small group of frequent buyers, while a large group has not bought in over 180 days.
4. **Revenue leakage.** 9.4% of booked sales (₹62.55 L) was cancelled or returned. The highest rates are in Fashion returns and among COD orders.
5. **Slowing growth.** Year-on-year revenue growth fell from 2024 to 2025. Central's share of revenue is shrinking.

## 14. Recommendations for Next Analytical Steps
**RFM analysis (Step 5)**
- How many customers fall into champion, loyal, at-risk and lapsed groups?
- How much revenue sits with customers who have not bought in over 180 days?
- Is the revenue concentration seen here driven by Frequency, by Monetary value, or by both?

**Customer segmentation (Step 6, K-Means)**
- Do natural behavioural clusters exist beyond RFM rules, for example discount-driven buyers or premium Electronics buyers?
- Do the clusters differ by age band, since age was the only demographic associated with revenue?

**CLV (Step 7)**
- What is each customer's expected future value?
- Does including profit margin change which customers look most valuable, given the Electronics vs Fashion margin gap?

**Revenue prediction (Step 8)**
- Can a customer's next-6-month revenue be predicted from past frequency, AOV, category mix, discount use and return rate?
- Behavioural variables showed strong associations here (e.g. order count rho ≈ 0.84 with revenue).
- Avoid leakage: build all features from the observation window only.

**Sales forecasting (Step 9)**
- The model needs to capture the upward trend, the growth slowdown and a strong, recurring Q4/October peak.
- Diwali's shifting date moves some festive demand between October and November. Test seasonal models (Holt-Winters, SARIMA) against a seasonal-naive baseline on a 6-month holdout.

## Limitations
- The data are synthetic. The patterns reflect the generator's assumptions, and the findings illustrate the method rather than describe a real market.
- 36 months give only 3 observations per calendar month, so seasonal estimates are indicative.
- The trend-adjusted seasonal ratio uses only 2 observations per month.
- Returned lines are excluded from revenue, and their logistics costs are not modelled.
- The bulk-order and premium outliers were kept on purpose. Mean-based statistics are therefore sensitive to them, so medians are reported alongside means.
