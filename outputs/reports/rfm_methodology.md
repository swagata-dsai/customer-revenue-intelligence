# RFM Methodology & Results: Customer Revenue Intelligence

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** This report is generated by `src/rfm.py` from `data/processed/customer_sales_cleaned.csv`, the Step 3 output. Every number is computed.

## 1. Objective
The aim is to turn 18,670 transaction lines into one row per customer that describes:
- **how recently** each customer bought;
- **how often** they bought;
- **how much** they spent.

These three measures are then used to find the most valuable, the most active and the at-risk customers, and to measure how concentrated revenue is. The result is the analytical foundation for K-Means segmentation (Step 6).

## 2. Reference Date
**Reference date = latest Order_Date in the data + 1 day = 2026-01-01**, since the latest order is dated 2025-12-31.

**Why this date:**
- It is derived from the data, not typed in.
- It describes the business as of the end of the extract.
- It gives the most recent buyer a Recency of 1 rather than 0.

The date is stored in `data/processed/customer_rfm_metadata.json`.

## 3. Definitions

| Measure | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Recency (R)** | Reference date − date of last **delivered** purchase, in days | Lower = more recent |
| **Frequency (F)** | Number of **distinct Order_IDs** with at least one Delivered line | Not row count. One order can contain several products; 1290 customers have more lines than orders. |
| **Monetary (M)** | Sum of **Sales** (₹, after discount) on Delivered lines | Reconciles exactly to Step 4 total revenue (₹6.01 Cr) |

### Treatment of cancelled and returned orders
- Only **Delivered** lines count as a purchase.
- Cancelled lines (never shipped) and Returned lines (refunded) are excluded from R, F and M. Their combined booked value of ₹62.55 L is not counted.
- Each customer's cancelled and returned line counts are kept in the RFM table as context.
- **16 customers** have no delivered purchase at all, because every line they placed was cancelled or returned. They have no valid Recency or Monetary value, so they are **excluded from scoring** and listed in `outputs/tables/rfm_excluded_customers.csv`. Scored plus excluded customers add up to 2,000.

## 5. RFM Scoring Method
Each measure is converted to a 1-5 score based on quintiles (5 = best):
- **R_Score** is reversed: fewer days since the last purchase gives a higher score.
- **F_Score** and **M_Score**: more orders or more spend gives a higher score.

**Checking the quintile method first.** Frequency has only 29 distinct values, and 16.2% of customers share the most common value. This was checked before using `pd.qcut`:
| Metric | Unique_Values | Quintile_Edges | Duplicate_Edges | Plain_qcut_OK | Largest_Tie_Group_% | Agreement_with_tie_aware_% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recency | 513 | 1, 38, 83, 162, 254, 1,096 | 0 | True | 1.3 | 98.8 |
| Frequency | 29 | 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 29 | 0 | True | 16.2 | 93.2 |
| Monetary | 1984 | 238, 4,707, 9,522, 19,911, 43,278, 417,800 | 0 | True | 0.1 | 99.9 |

**Result.** For this dataset, the quintile edges are unique for all three measures, so `pd.qcut(q=5)` is used. `qcut` keeps identical values in the same score.

The code also contains a **robust fallback**, used automatically if a future dataset produces duplicate edges:
- compute each customer's percentile rank, with tied values averaged;
- score = ceil(5 × percentile rank).

This fallback agrees with qcut for 93-99.9% of customers here (last column above).

Frequency's score bands are unequal in size (for example, all 321 customers with exactly 3 orders share F = 2). This is the honest consequence of tied values.

**Combined scores**

| Field | Formula | Use |
|---|---|---|
| RFM_Cell | R, F and M digits joined, e.g. "545" | Look-up label, not arithmetic |
| RFM_Score | R + F + M (3-15) | Single ranking score. **Equal weights**, because nothing in the data justifies weighting one dimension more than another. |
| FM_Score | (F + M) / 2 | Value score used in the segment rules. F and M are strongly correlated (Spearman ρ = 0.84), so averaging them avoids counting the same information twice. |

## 6. Segment Rules (rule-based, exhaustive, evaluated top-down)
| Segment | RFM Characteristics (rule) | Business Meaning | Value-Activity Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| Champions | R 4-5 and FM >= 4.5 | Bought recently, buy often and spend the most | Active high value |
| Loyal Customers | R 3-5 and FM 3.5-4 (or R 3 and FM >= 4.5) | Regular, valuable buyers; strong relationship | Active high value |
| Potential Loyalists | R 4-5 and FM 2.5-3 | Recent buyers with average frequency and spend | Developing |
| Recent Customers | R 5 and FM < 2.5 | Bought very recently but only a little so far | Developing |
| Promising | R 4 and FM < 2.5 | Fairly recent, low frequency and spend | Developing |
| Needs Attention | R 3 and FM 2.5-3 | Average customers whose recency is slipping | Slipping |
| About to Sleep | R 3 and FM < 2.5 | Below-average value and going quiet | Slipping |
| Can't Lose Them | R 1-2 and FM >= 4.5 | Were among the best customers; have not bought for a long time | Slipping |
| At Risk | R 1-2 and FM 3-4 | Valuable in the past, now inactive | Slipping |
| Hibernating | R 2 and FM < 3 | Low value and long inactive | Inactive |
| Lost | R 1 and FM < 3 | Lowest recency, frequency and spend | Inactive |

- The rules cover every (R, FM) combination, so each customer receives **exactly one** segment.
- The segments are grouped into four value-activity groups, used for charts and summaries.

- Gini coefficient: **0.611**.
- 50% of revenue comes from 12.0% of customers.
- 80% of revenue comes from **34.1%** of customers.

Revenue is clearly concentrated, but the data does **not** show a literal 80/20 Pareto rule.

## 8. Outlier Considerations
- No customers were removed. The largest spenders, up to ₹4.18 L, are the Champions that RFM is meant to find.
- Quintile scores are rank-based, so extreme values do not distort the score boundaries.
- Averages by segment are sensitive to extremes, so medians are also saved in `rfm_segment_summary.csv`.
- The 19 bulk-order lines identified in Step 3 are included, because they are genuine purchases.

## 9. Limitations
1. **Quintile scores are relative.** A score of 5 means top 20% of this customer base, not an absolute level of value. The thresholds will move if the data changes.
2. **The segment rules are judgement-based.** The cut-offs (for example FM ≥ 4.5 for Champions) are transparent and conventional, but they are not learnt from the data. Customers near a boundary could reasonably sit in either segment.
3. **Monetary is revenue, not profit.** Segment profit margins (in the summary table) range from about 20% to 35%, so revenue-based value overstates Electronics-heavy customers. CLV in Step 7 will adjust for margin.
4. **Recency is measured from a single snapshot date**, and Frequency and Monetary cover the full 36 months. Customers acquired recently have had less time to build Frequency.
5. **The data are synthetic.** The segments illustrate the method and do not describe a real customer base.

## 10. RFM Rule-Based Segmentation vs Machine-Learning Segmentation

| | RFM rules (this step) | K-Means (Step 6) |
|---|---|---|
| How groups are formed | Human-defined rules on quintile scores | An algorithm groups customers that are close together in feature space |
| Inputs | R, F, M scores only | Scaled (log-transformed) R, F, M and possibly other behavioural features |
| Number of groups | Fixed by the rule table (11 segments) | Chosen with the elbow method and silhouette score |
| Explainability | Very high: every rule is written down | Needs cluster profiling to interpret |
| Weakness | Boundaries are arbitrary | Results depend on scaling, feature choice and k |

The two approaches are complementary. RFM gives an immediately usable business vocabulary. K-Means tests whether the data's natural structure agrees with these rules.

## 11. Business Interpretation
- **Champions:** About one customer in six generates over half of all revenue. 334 customers (16.8%) -> ₹3.23 Cr (53.8% of revenue); avg recency 33 days, avg 15.4 orders, avg spend ₹96,797. *Interpretation:* Protect with loyalty benefits and early access; losing even a few would hurt disproportionately.
- **Loyal Customers:** Second value tier, slightly less recent. 240 customers (12.1%) -> ₹85.79 L (14.3% of revenue); avg recency 91 days, avg 7.2 orders, avg spend ₹35,745. *Interpretation:* Upgrade path to Champions: cross-sell categories and reward frequency.
- **Potential Loyalists:** Recent, mid-value buyers. 152 customers (7.7%) -> ₹21.91 L (3.6% of revenue); avg recency 47 days, avg 4.2 orders, avg spend ₹14,418. *Interpretation:* Best growth pool: onboarding and second-category offers could move them up.
- **At Risk / Can't Lose Them:** Former high-value customers who have gone quiet. At Risk: 263 customers (13.3%) -> ₹67.63 L (11.3% of revenue); avg recency 259 days, avg 5.9 orders, avg spend ₹25,717. Can't Lose Them: 79 customers (4.0%) -> ₹42.85 L (7.1% of revenue); avg recency 228 days, avg 9.8 orders, avg spend ₹54,244. *Interpretation:* Priority win-back targets: the revenue they used to generate is large.
- **High-value at risk (rule):** Top-40% spenders in the bottom-40% of recency. 229 customers, ₹95.10 L historical revenue (15.8%); median recency 226 days. *Interpretation:* A concrete Customer_ID list for a reactivation campaign (Step 7 CLV will size expected value).
- **Inactive customers:** Hibernating + Lost are a large share of customers but little revenue. 449 customers (22.6%) -> 4.5% of revenue. *Interpretation:* Low-cost automated reactivation only; do not spend premium retention budget here.
- **Revenue Concentration:** Revenue is concentrated, but less than an 80/20 rule. Top 20% -> 64.3%; 80% of revenue comes from 34.1% of customers; Gini 0.611. *Interpretation:* A focused key-account style programme for the top fifth is justified.
- **Repeat Customers:** Almost all buyers come back at least once. Repeat (2+ orders): 89.4%; one-time: 10.6%. *Interpretation:* Retention is working at a basic level; the lever is frequency and value, not first repeat.
- **Frequency vs Monetary:** Frequency and Monetary move together. Spearman rho(F, M) = 0.84. *Interpretation:* F and M carry overlapping information - one reason the segments use FM = (F+M)/2, and a point to handle (scaling / feature choice) in K-Means.
- **Recency:** Recency separates recently active customers from a long-lapsed group. Median recency 121 days; 42.5% bought in the last 90 days; 34.9% not for 180+ days. *Interpretation:* Recency is the earliest warning sign of a customer going quiet, and the one to monitor monthly - this project reports inactivity, never churn, because the data defines no churn event.
