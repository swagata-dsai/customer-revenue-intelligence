## Step 5: RFM Customer Analysis

**Methodology** (details in `outputs/reports/rfm_methodology.md`):
- **Reference date:** the latest Order_Date + 1 day = **2026-01-01**. It is derived from the data and stored in `data/processed/customer_rfm_metadata.json`.
- **Recency:** days since the customer's last **delivered** purchase.
- **Frequency:** distinct `Order_ID`s with at least one Delivered line.
- **Monetary:** delivered Sales in ₹. This reconciles to the Step 4 revenue of ₹6.01 Cr.
- **Cancelled and returned lines** are excluded. 16 customers with no delivered purchase are excluded from scoring and listed separately.
- **Scoring:** `pd.qcut` quintiles (1-5; Recency reversed). If quintile edges ever tie, the code falls back automatically to a tie-aware percentile rank.
- **Combined scores:** RFM_Score = R + F + M (equal weights), and FM_Score = (F + M) / 2.

**Customer-level analysis.** Distributions of R, F and M (raw and log-transformed), repeat rate, activity, revenue concentration (top 1/5/10/20%, Gini, customer share needed for 80% of revenue), and ID-only lists of top customers and high-value at-risk customers.

**Segment creation.** Eleven transparent, exhaustive rules on R_Score and FM_Score assign exactly one segment per customer:
- Active high value: Champions, Loyal Customers.
- Developing: Potential Loyalists, Recent Customers, Promising.
- Slipping: Needs Attention, About to Sleep, Can't Lose Them, At Risk.
- Inactive: Hibernating, Lost.

This is **rule-based** segmentation. K-Means (Step 6) is separate.

| Headline (synthetic data) | Value |
|---|---|
| Champions | 16.8% of customers, 53.8% of revenue |
| Champions + Loyal Customers | 28.9% of customers, 68.1% of revenue |
| High-value customers at risk (M_Score ≥ 4, R_Score ≤ 2) | 229 customers, ₹95.10 L (15.8%) |
| Top 20% of customers | 64.3% of revenue (not an 80/20 split) |
| Repeat customers | 89.4% |

## Machine Learning Customer Segmentation (Step 6)

Step 5 grouped customers with fixed **rules** (RFM scores). Step 6 lets an unsupervised algorithm, **K-Means**, find the groups from the data itself, then compares the two. The Step 5 RFM columns are kept unchanged; the ML cluster is added as separate columns (`Cluster_ID`, `Cluster_Label`).

**Why K-Means.** The inputs are three continuous, numeric customer measures, and the goal is a small number of groups that a business user can read. K-Means fits that: it is fast, deterministic with a fixed seed, and each cluster has a centre that can be translated back into rupees and days. Its limitations are stated in the report: it assumes roughly round clusters of similar spread, and it always returns K groups even when the data has no sharp boundaries.

**Features used: Recency, Frequency, Monetary** (per customer, from `customer_rfm_segments.csv`). The decisions are evidence-based (`ml_feature_selection.csv`, `ml_feature_set_comparison.csv`):
- Total_Quantity (ρ = 0.94 with Frequency) and Categories_Bought (ρ = 0.86) were left out because they repeat Frequency.
- Tenure_Days was left out because it is cut off by the data window.
- Adding Average_Order_Value or Average_Discount lowered the silhouette at K = 4, from 0.353 to 0.273 and 0.286.
- Customer_ID, names, raw categorical identifiers and any future prediction targets are **not** model inputs.

**Preprocessing.** All three features are right-skewed, so each goes through `log1p` and then `StandardScaler`. Both steps live in one scikit-learn `Pipeline`, saved as `models/rfm_scaler.pkl`. Without scaling, Monetary (in rupees) would dominate the distances.

**Choosing K.** K = 2 to 10 were each fitted with `random_state=42` and scored on:
- inertia (elbow),
- silhouette,
- Calinski-Harabasz,
- Davies-Bouldin,
- smallest-cluster share,
- seed stability (mean ARI across 10 seeds).

The documented rule: K ≥ 3 (business usefulness), smallest cluster ≥ 5%, stability ARI ≥ 0.90, then the highest silhouette. **K = 4** is the only K ≥ 3 that passes the stability test: ARI 0.95, compared with 0.79 at K = 3 and 0.60 at K = 5. It is also where the elbow bends, and it has the lowest Davies-Bouldin score of all K tested (see `ml_k_evaluation.csv`).

**Evaluation of the final model (K = 4)**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Inertia | 1,780.25 |
| Silhouette | 0.353 |
| Calinski-Harabasz | 1,546.6 |
| Davies-Bouldin | 0.951 |
| Seed stability (mean ARI, 10 seeds) | 0.95 |
| Smallest cluster | 18.3% of customers |
| PCA variance, PC1 + PC2 (visualisation only) | 95.1% |

A silhouette of 0.35 means the clusters are real but overlap at their edges; 40 of the 1,984 customers have a negative silhouette.

**Clusters found.** The labels come from rules on cluster medians, not hand-assigned:
- value is median Monetary compared with the all-customer terciles;
- activity is median Recency: Active ≤ 83 days, Inactive ≥ 255 days, otherwise Lapsing.

| Cluster | Label | Customers | Revenue share | Median R / F / M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | High-Value, Active | 364 (18.3%) | 55.7% | 28 d / 13 / ₹71,549 |
| 1 | Low-Value, Active | 453 (22.8%) | 5.0% | 57 d / 3 / ₹5,945 |
| 2 | Mid-Value, Lapsing | 753 (38.0%) | 35.4% | 168 d / 6 / ₹22,205 |
| 3 | Low-Value, Inactive | 414 (20.9%) | 3.9% | 624 d / 2 / ₹4,392 |

**RFM vs ML.** The agreement is moderate: ARI = 0.40 and NMI = 0.58 between the 11 RFM segments and the 4 clusters. Both methods agree at the extremes. The main difference is that most RFM "Loyal Customers" (R score 3) fall into the K-Means "Mid-Value, Lapsing" cluster. See `rfm_vs_ml_cluster*.csv` and `rfm_vs_ml_cluster_heatmap.png`.

## Customer Lifetime Value (Step 7)

Step 7 measures what each customer **has been** worth and estimates what each customer **may be** worth over the next 12 months. The two are kept strictly apart:

| | Observed historical value | Estimated future value |
|---|---|---|
| Columns | `Historical_Revenue_CLV`, `Historical_Profit_CLV` | `Estimated_12M_Future_Profit` (+ `Illustrative_3Y_Value`) |
| Nature | Fact: Delivered revenue / profit 2023-2025, reconciles to the transactions | Model estimate under stated assumptions, not a guarantee |

**Methodology**
- **Historical CLV:** total Delivered revenue and profit per customer (profit = revenue - cost). Profit CLV is the main measure. Totals: ₹6.01 Cr revenue, ₹1.40 Cr profit (23.3% margin); median profit per customer ₹3,893. The top 20% of customers hold 59.3% of profit.
- **Data readiness was checked first** (`clv_data_readiness.csv`). Repeat purchasing, frequency and 36 months of history support prediction. Churn is **not** observable: 41-56% of customers inactive for 180+ days bought again within a year. So a 180-day rule only flags customers as *potentially at-risk*, never churned.
- **Probabilistic models:** BG/NBD was implemented with scipy and tested on 2025 (88% of hold-out orders predicted); it is kept as a benchmark. Gamma-Gamma was **rejected** because spend per order and purchase frequency are correlated (Spearman 0.39).
- **Predictive CLV (time-based, no leakage):**

| Panel | Feature period | Target period | Customers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Train | 2023 | 2024 | 1,137 |
| Test (out-of-time) | 2024 | 2025 | 1,328 |
| Scoring | 2025 | next 12 months (estimate) | 1,716 |

  Target = Delivered profit in the 12 months after the cutoff. Customers with no order in 2025 (268) get the observed average next-year profit of the equivalent group at the 2025 cutoff (₹830).

**Features.** Eight 12-month behaviour features: Recency_Days, Frequency, AOV, Profit_Margin, Avg_Discount, Categories, Tenure_Days_Capped and Return_Rate.
- Revenue, Profit and profit per order were excluded as exact products of AOV, Frequency and margin.
- Quantity and Purchase_Rate were excluded for VIF > 5.
- RFM segments and cluster labels are **not** features, because they were computed on the full period.
- The features are preprocessed with arcsinh on the skewed ones, then StandardScaler (`models/clv_preprocessor.pkl`).

**Model.** Ridge regression (interpretable baseline), Random Forest and Gradient Boosting were compared. The selection rule is the lowest 5-fold CV RMSE on the training year, and **Random Forest** won. The same specification was refit on the 2024 and 2025 targets for scoring (`models/clv_model.pkl`).

**Evaluation (out-of-time test, 2024 behaviour → 2025 profit)**

| Method | MAE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random forest (selected) | ₹2,793 | ₹4,269 | 0.270 |
| Ridge baseline | ₹2,853 | ₹4,469 | 0.201 |
| BG/NBD × avg profit per order | ₹2,693 | ₹4,356 | 0.241 |
| Simplified formula AOV × F × margin × P(repeat) | ₹2,859 | ₹4,606 | 0.151 |
| Naive (last year repeats) | ₹2,947 | ₹4,648 | 0.135 |

- **Ranking:** the top 20% of customers by predicted value captured 41.5% of actual 2025 profit.
- **Bias:** the predicted total was 90% of the actual total.
- **MAPE:** not used as a headline metric, because 6.7% of actual values are zero; WAPE is reported instead.
- **Accuracy is modest:** use the estimates to **rank** customers, not as exact rupee forecasts.

**Output files**
- **Data:** `data/processed/customer_value_history.csv`, `customer_clv.csv` and `clv_modeling_dataset.csv`.
- **Models:** `models/clv_model.pkl`, `models/clv_preprocessor.pkl`, `models/clv_model_metadata.json`.
- **Tables:** 26 `outputs/tables/clv_*.csv` files. They include `clv_by_rfm_segment.csv`, `clv_by_ml_cluster.csv`, `clv_business_insights.csv`, `clv_model_comparison.csv` and `clv_validation_checks.csv`.
- **Figures:** 13 in `outputs/figures/`, named `clv_*.png`, `customer_tenure_vs_value.png`, `frequency_vs_clv.png` and `recency_vs_clv.png`.
- **Report:** `outputs/reports/clv_analysis_report.md`.
- **Notebook:** `notebooks/05_customer_lifetime_value.ipynb` (executed).

**Limitations**
- The data is synthetic.
- Per-customer accuracy is modest (R² 0.27).
- Activity grew year on year and there is only one hold-out year.
- Tenure is left-censored for the 38.7% of customers who existed before 2023.
- There is no churn label.
- Customers inactive for 12+ months get a group-level estimate.
- The 3-year value is illustrative, undiscounted and not validated.
- Feature importance shows association, not causation.

## Revenue Prediction (Step 8)

**Question.** Can an existing customer's revenue next quarter be predicted from what was known before the quarter started, and which measurable characteristics does the model associate with it?

**Prediction setup**

| Item | Definition |
|---|---|
| Prediction unit | Customer × quarter: every customer with ≥ 1 Delivered order in the 12 months before a quarter start |
| Target | `Next_Quarter_Revenue` = Delivered Sales (₹) in the 3 months after the cutoff (0 if no purchase; 54% of rows) |
| Features | 12-month (and 3-month) history before the cutoff: customer, product, regional and calendar-quarter features |
| Train / validation / test | targets 2024-Q1..Q4 / 2025-Q1..Q2 / 2025-Q3..Q4 (8 quarterly cutoffs, chronological, no shuffling) |
| Coverage | 82–91% of each quarter's revenue (new or reactivated customers are outside the panel) |

**Modelling approach**
- **Leakage audit:** every feature window ends the day before the cutoff. Step 5–7 labels (RFM segment, cluster, CLV) are full-period, so they are excluded as features and used only to group errors.
- **Feature selection (training rows only):**
  - exact identities were removed (Rev_12M = AOV × Orders; Profit_12M = AOV × Orders × margin);
  - then the non-core feature with the highest VIF above 10 was dropped, repeatedly.
- **Result:** 19 numeric + 5 categorical features.
- **Design note:** revenue *levels* (company / region ₹) were tried first and replaced by shares and ratios, because they acted as a time trend (validation bias −95%). This is documented in the report.
- **Pipeline:** a scikit-learn `ColumnTransformer` fitted on training rows only. Numeric features get median imputation, arcsinh on skewed features, then StandardScaler; categorical features get one-hot encoding.
- **Models tested:**
  - baselines: previous quarter, same quarter last year, 12-month quarterly mean;
  - Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting;
  - Random Forest tuned with a 15-setting randomized search, fitted on train and scored on validation.
- **Selection rule (fixed in advance):** keep models within 2% of the best validation RMSE, then choose the simplest model type.

**Evaluation (test = 2025-Q3 + Q4, 2,913 customer-quarters)**

| Model | MAE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline: 12-month quarterly mean | ₹5,436 | ₹11,466 | 0.016 |
| Baseline: previous quarter | ₹6,423 | ₹14,483 | −0.570 |
| **Linear regression (final)** | **₹5,658** | **₹11,232** | **0.056** |
| Random forest | ₹5,762 | ₹11,265 | 0.050 |
| Gradient boosting | ₹5,845 | ₹11,383 | 0.030 |
| Random forest (tuned) | ₹5,554 | ₹11,148 | 0.070 |

- **Final model: Linear Regression.** Its validation RMSE is within 1% of the best, it has almost no overfitting (the tree models' train–validation R² gaps are 0.4–0.5), its coefficients are readable, and it is cheap to run.
- **Accuracy is low at customer level:** R² is 0.06 and the mean baseline has a lower MAE. Most customers buy 0–1 times a quarter.
- **Totals:** the two-quarter total bias was +1.1%, but single quarters missed by −19% and +19%. Seasonality learned from one year did not carry over.
- **Important features** (permutation importance on test): Orders_12M ≫ Main_Category, category mix, AOV_12M, Categories_12M. These are **associations, not causes**.
- **Model-based scenarios** (not guaranteed outcomes): frequency +10% → +5.5% predicted revenue; AOV +10% → +0.6%; discount −2 pp → +1.3%.

**Output files**

- **Models:** `models/revenue_prediction_model.pkl` (the full pipeline), `models/revenue_prediction_preprocessor.pkl` and `models/revenue_model_metadata.json`.

## Sales Forecasting (Step 9)

**Time-series forecasting** of total monthly revenue (Delivered Sales), built on the Step 3 cleaned data.

**Design** (decided from the data)
- **Frequency:** monthly. All 36 months (2023-01..2025-12) and all 1,096 days have sales.
- **Split (chronological):** train 2023-01..2024-12 (24 months), validation 2025-01..06 (6), test 2025-07..12 (6).
- **Horizon:** 6 months (2026-01..2026-06), the same horizon that was evaluated. There isn't enough history (3 seasonal cycles) to justify 12 months.

**EDA findings**
- **Trend:** strong upward trend, about +₹53k per month. Annual growth was +51% (2024) and +38% (2025).
- **Seasonality:** a recurring October / Q4 peak; other months are inconsistent.
- **Stationarity (ADF):** the level is non-stationary (p = 0.20); the first difference is stationary (p = 0.007).

**Models tested**
- **Baselines:** naive (last month) and seasonal naive (same month last year).
- **Exponential smoothing:** 6 ETS configurations, including Holt-Winters.
- **ARIMA / SARIMA:** 6 configurations with d = 1, guided by the ADF, ACF and PACF.
- **Theta method:** the optional extra model. Prophet was not added.

**Evaluation methodology**
- **Choosing within each model type:** configurations are compared on the validation months.
- **Test:** each model is refitted on train + validation and evaluated on the 6 test months.
- **Backtest:** an expanding-window rolling-origin backtest with 7 folds, each forecasting 6 months ahead.
- **Metrics:** MAE, RMSE, MAPE (no month has zero revenue), sMAPE, WAPE, bias and interval coverage.
- **Selection rule (fixed in advance):** lowest mean backtest MAPE among models that beat **both** baselines on test MAE.

| Model (best configuration) | Test MAE | Test MAPE | Backtest mean MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive | ₹2.58 L | 10.1% | 18.5% |
| Seasonal naive | ₹6.19 L | 25.6% | 33.4% |
| Damped Holt (ETS) | ₹2.73 L | 9.8% | 13.1% |
| ARIMA(1,1,1) + drift | ₹2.61 L | 10.5% | 13.5% |
| **Theta (selected)** | **₹2.13 L** | **7.8%** | **14.6%** |

- **Why Theta:** it is the only model that beat naive on the test window. The top three models are close.
- **Seasonality:** Theta's own seasonality test did not flag 12-month seasonality, so it forecasts level + trend. That is fine for January–June, but not for Q4.

**Future forecast**
- January–June 2026 totals about ₹1.58 Cr, with 80% and 95% prediction intervals that widen over the horizon.

## SQL Business Analytics (Step 10)

**Database technology:** SQLite (via Python's built-in `sqlite3`; no server to install).
**Database location:** `data/customer_revenue_intelligence.db`, rebuilt from the processed files. This replaces Step 3's `data/processed/revenue_intelligence.db`, so there is one source of truth.

**Schema** (`sql/schema.sql`): 11 tables with primary keys, 9 foreign keys and CHECK constraints for domains and business rules, plus 2 views.

| Layer | Tables |
|---|---|
| Transactional core (Steps 2-3) | `locations` → `customers` → `orders` → `order_items` ← `products` |
| Analytics layer (outputs of Steps 5-9) | `customer_rfm` → `rfm_segment_definitions`; `customer_segments` → `ml_clusters`; `customer_value` (CLV); `revenue_forecasts` |
| Views | `vw_sales_flat` (all lines, original layout); `vw_delivered_sales` (the Delivered-only revenue rule) |

The ER diagram is in `outputs/figures/sql/er_diagram.png`.

**Major SQL analyses** (`sql/analysis_queries.sql`, 37 queries)
- **Revenue:** KPIs, year, quarter, growth, margin.
- **Customers:** top customers by revenue / profit / frequency / AOV, new vs existing, cohorts, frequency bands, inactivity (a reference date computed in SQL).
- **Revenue concentration:** window-function Pareto with rank, cumulative revenue and cumulative %.
- **RFM and ML segments:** analysed against the transactions; the segments are read from Steps 5-6, not recreated in SQL.
- **Products:** top products by revenue / profit / quantity, category and sub-category, product profitability, average selling price, discount bands, products to investigate.
- **Regions:** revenue, profit, orders, customers, AOV, category-by-region matrix, top states.
- **Time:** monthly metrics with MoM / YoY (`LAG`), best / worst months, year-to-date running totals, month-of-year pattern.

**Business questions** (`sql/business_queries.sql`, BQ01–BQ20)
- Each question states its business question, analytical purpose and how to read the result. Examples:
  - top customers;
  - segment contribution;
  - high-revenue / low-margin products;
  - high-value customers with low recent activity;
  - discount vs margin within each category;
  - share of revenue from high-value customers;
  - retention and new-customer dynamics;
  - time between orders;
  - forecast vs actual.
- **Techniques used:** INNER / LEFT JOIN, CASE WHEN, CTEs, subqueries, window functions (RANK, ROW_NUMBER, NTILE, running totals, `SUM() OVER ()` shares, LAG), HAVING, date functions and UNION ALL.

**Validation** (`outputs/tables/sql_validation_checks.csv`, 56 checks)
- **Counts:** row counts for every table against the source files; unique customers and products.
- **Totals vs pandas:** revenue, profit, cost, quantity and orders.
- **Integrity:** NULLs in required columns, duplicate primary keys, orphan foreign keys (explicit `LEFT JOIN`s plus `PRAGMA foreign_key_check`).
- **Cross-step:** SQL recomputations match Step 5 RFM, Step 6 cluster revenue, Step 7 CLV profit and Step 9 monthly actuals.

**Outputs**
- **Query results:** `outputs/tables/sql/<query>.csv` (57 files).
- **Named tables:** `outputs/tables/product_profitability.csv`, `customer_segment_sql_analysis.csv` and `customer_revenue_concentration.csv`.
- **Catalogues and checks:** `sql_query_catalog.csv`, `sql_technique_coverage.csv`, `sql_key_findings.csv`, `sql_table_catalog.csv` and `sql_quality_checklist.csv`.
- **Figures:** `outputs/figures/sql/`, the ER diagram plus 11 SQL-driven charts.
